Records · Registros

Artifacts

Los papeles, no la novela.

These are records behind the work, not the work itself. A verified record is an original or an official text that has been read. Family narrative is what the Chronicles and family memory say, and it stays labeled as that. Fiction is the dialogue and the scenes in the novel and the plays: a document does not rewrite a scene, and a scene is not offered here as a record.

The papers are not all one story, and they are not all flattering. They show grants and baptisms, signed letters and death certificates. They also show conflict: crown and state, the church, the colonists, and the Native peoples already living on this land. Settling new country was discovery and disruption at the same time. It was innovative, great, and awful, depending on who is looking. The settlers conquered, lived that life, and left paper that carries both the fact of conquering peoples and the richness of the land and its water, grass, and ore. The page says that plainly. Judgment belongs to the reader.

  • Verified record Registro verificado. The text has been read, or it is a printed official copy of a statute or deed.
  • Catalog record Registro de catálogo. A finding aid names the item. The instrument itself has not been read, and nothing inside it is quoted.
  • Family narrative Narrativa familiar. What the family account says. It is kept, and it is not dressed up as a certificate.

III

Stephenson / Azcárate of El Paso

Stephenson · Azcárate de El Paso

The Ysleta pueblo grant and El Rancho de Ascarete are both printed statutes. They are shown the same way: the page, the date, the credit, and what the act does and does not prove.

Approved 11 February 1858

Texas confirmation of El Rancho de Ascarete

Chapter 120 of the Seventh Legislature relinquishes the state’s right in three leagues called El Rancho de Ascarete to Juan and Jacinto Ascarete, as the act spells the name, their heirs and assigns, to be surveyed and patented, and it preserves rights that already existed.

Citation: H. P. N. Gammel, The Laws of Texas, vol. 4, p. 1027. General Land Office file locator BEX 1–001390, surname index, letter A. Credit: UNT Libraries, The Portal to Texas History.

Verified record Show image Public-domain statute

Approved 31 January 1854

Texas relinquishment to the inhabitants of Ysleta

After the Rio Grande changed course in 1831 or 1832, chapter XXX relinquishes about two leagues on the east bank, formerly of the Cinecue town tract, to the inhabitants of Ysleta, and that bank later became the south line of the Ascarate survey.

Citation: Gammel, vol. 4, p. 42 and p. 43. Credit: UNT Libraries, The Portal to Texas History. A later act recites the date as 21 January 1854. That conflict is in the printed laws.

Verified record Show image Public-domain statute

15 February 1858

Amendment of the Ysleta relinquishment

The 1858 amendment changes the 1854 relinquishment, and the printed laws recite the original approval as 21 January 1854, which does not match the 31 January date on the 1854 act.

Citation: Gammel, vol. 4, pp. 1094–1095. Credit: UNT Libraries, The Portal to Texas History.

Verified record Show image Public-domain statute

16 November 1854

Reciprocal Bracito deeds: García y San Juan and Hugh Stephenson

Printed official copies show Francisco García y San Juan conveying two-thirds of Bracito, recited as 13,462 acres more or less, to Hugh Stephenson for $1,000, and Stephenson releasing the remaining third, recited as 6,731 acres more or less, back to García after a court partition. Bracito was not an original grant to Hugh.

Citation: U.S. House Report 321, 36th Congress, 1st session (1860), University of Oklahoma, SHAREOK. Acknowledged 17 November 1854 and recorded 12 March 1855. Credit: University of Oklahoma Libraries, SHAREOK.

Verified record Show image Public-domain report

5 March 1914, rehearing 23 April 1914; appeal 24 February 1916

Stevens v. Crosby and Crosby v. Stevens

The opinions recite the 1854 Ysleta act, the 1858 Ascarate act, the 1886 Randolph survey, and a patent of 14 September 1886 to Juan and Jacinto Ascarate, and they hold that the 1854 bank of the Río Viejo, not later course-and-distance calls, is the dividing line.

Citation: 166 S.W. 62, Stevens v. Crosby and Crosby v. Stevens. Credit: the opinions are public. Editorial headnotes are not copied. The parties are later title holders, not proved here to be Azcárate heirs, and the 1886 patent date is taken from the opinion, not from an inspection of General Land Office file BEX 1–001390.

V

Conflicts in the record

Conflictos en el registro

This section is a board of conflicts, not a hidden footnote and not a case against anyone. The captions stay short. The sources are named. The grants, the water, the church, the colonists, and the Native towns are all on the paper. A pueblo grant and a private ranch grant that meet at the same riverbank are shown with equal respect. Where the documents do not give a Native voice, none is invented.

Principal papers 2 November 1734. Index span 1719 to 2 February 1735.

Settlers’ letters to the viceroy, with copies of royal edicts

A cataloged dossier of about twenty pages holds settlers’ letters and copies of royal edicts about water, imprisonment, refusal of medical travel, and a priest’s fees, and Vicente is not named on this index’s list of persons.

Citation: Archivo General de la Nación, México, Provincias Internas, vol. 236, ff. 244–253, indexed at the University of Arizona. Credit: AGN, via the Documentary Relations of the Southwest. No image is attached to the opened catalog record.

Documents 1771–1783. English translations 1927.

Protests against mission land claims

Vicente and Juan Andrés Álvarez Travieso protest mission land claims in papers that the Handbook of Texas connects with Rancho de las Mulas, a quarrel between colonists and the missions over land, kept here separate from the water fight of the 1730s, and Native testimony is not in hand until the file is read.

Citation: Briscoe Center collection; Texas Archival Resources Online; Handbook of Texas. Credit: Briscoe Center. The 1927 translation is not hosted. Vicente died in 1779, so later papers in the span may be by heirs or by other people. No manuscript image was obtained.

River change recited as 1831 or 1832. Acts of 1854 and 1858. Appeals of 1914 and 1916.

Río Viejo: the Ysleta grant and El Rancho de Ascarete

The 1854 act says the inhabitants of Ysleta lost part of a grant from the government of Spain when the Rio Grande moved, leaving part of the Cinecue town tract on the east bank, and the 1914 opinion holds that the 1854 bank of the Río Viejo, not later measurements, divides that tract from the Ascarate survey.

Citation: the statutes above, Ysleta and Ascarete, and the opinions in Stevens v. Crosby. Secondary context, not a substitute for the statute: Handbook of Texas, “Tigua Indians.” Credit: UNT Libraries for the statutes; CourtListener for the opinions; the Texas State Historical Association for the handbook. The legal parties of 1914 and 1916 are later title holders, not automatically the original families. No original crown grant to the Azcárate family was inspected, and none is displayed.
