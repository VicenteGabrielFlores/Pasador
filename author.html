Vicente Gabriel Flores

Writer · Poet · Playwright · Visual Artist

Vicente Gabriel Flores is the literary and artistic name of Gabriel Salcedo, a Tejano writer and visual artist based in Austin, Texas. His work moves between novels, poetry, dramatic writing, historical chronicles, and visual art. Across these forms runs a sustained interest in memory, inheritance, identity, displacement, and the passage of time.

At the center of much of his writing is a family history spanning nearly three centuries, two oceans, and five flags. Descended from Carlos F. Flores, who crossed the International Bridge from Ciudad Juárez into El Paso in November 1913, Flores writes from within a history that is at once familial and historical. Among his ancestors is Juana María Ascárate de Stephenson, the heroine of Concordia, inducted into the El Paso County Historical Society’s Hall of Honor in 1994. Her stone still names her “First Lady of Concordia,” and her grave, the first at the Stephenson ranch chapel on the Rio Grande, began what became El Paso’s Concordia Cemetery. The stories begin in Tenerife, pass through the founding of San Antonio and the turbulent history of the El Paso del Norte region, and extend into imagined futures far beyond the present.

This history forms the foundation of A King’s Promise, an evolving body of work encompassing novels, documentary chronicles, plays, and seventeen choral poems. Its companion works include PASADOR, Las Crónicas, The Harness-Maker’s Son, LA TOMA, The Modern Play, and THE MANIFEST. Together, they explore what is inherited, what is lost, what is carried forward, and what remains when the circumstances of history repeatedly change.

His work is not confined to historical narrative. His poetry moves between the intimate and the cosmological, finding connections between individual lives, generations, the natural world, and time itself. His visual art approaches similar questions through symbolic forms, abstraction, and a personal visual language. Writing and painting are not separate pursuits but different expressions of the same imaginative impulse.

Flores holds a Bachelor of Arts in Japanese and Chinese Language from the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa and an MBA from Rutgers Business School. He spent more than a decade working in asset management and capital markets across Japan, China, and Hong Kong before returning to Texas, where he continues his career in corporate contracts and mergers and acquisitions.

The work is offered to anyone who wishes to encounter it. Whether it finds a wide readership or remains something discovered by a few, its purpose is unchanged: to preserve something of a life, a family, a way of seeing, and a particular moment in time.

His hand. Now mine.

Su mano. Ahora la mía.
