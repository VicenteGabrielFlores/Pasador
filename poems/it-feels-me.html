A Poem · 2026

It Feels Me

Me siente

⊳  ✦  ⊴

The river takes my ankles first.
Then the sand goes out from under my heels,
grain by grain, toward the sea, toward later.

To the knees. To the waist.
My hands go looking for the bank.
The cold closes my throat.
The water takes my mouth.
I give it my mouth.
I open my eyes in it.

And the river takes my weight.
All of it. The bones I was born carrying,
the name, and every man and woman
who stood on this bank behind me.
It takes them, and gives back only current.

I am not water, says the river.
I am the floor of the world, falling.
Past the edge I run faster than light can swim.
Nothing has ever come back up me.

Two dark ones fell into each other
before there was a fish in any sea to hear it.
The ring of it reached this river one September before dawn,
thinner than a proton,
and went through my chest,
and through every child asleep along its banks,
and did not stop.

The water fills my ears and he is there,
ten years old, on the bridge,
a folded paper in his fist he will not open,
his father’s one word still in his ear: sigue.
And the same boy, old, on a porch on Florence Street,
lifting his hand.

I am the one who opened it.

And my son, old, on a bank I will never stand on,
lifting his hand.
Beside me. In the water.

Let go, says the river. I have all of them.

I feel the past. It feels me.
I feel the future. It feels me.

Siento el pasado. Me siente.
Siento el futuro. Me siente.
Me siente.
Siente.

And the sand goes out from under my heels,
toward the sea, toward later,
and I let go of the bottom,
and float.
